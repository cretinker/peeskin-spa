Why it happens

Tightly curled hair does not grow straight out of the follicle. It curves. When you shave, the blade cuts the hair at an angle and leaves a sharp tip, and as that tip grows it curls back and pierces the follicle wall from the inside — or never breaks the surface at all.

Your immune system treats the trapped hair as a foreign body. That is the bump: an inflammatory response, sometimes with pus, sometimes tender for days.

On deeper skin tones, inflammation almost always leaves pigment behind. That is why the underarm or bikini line ends up darker than the surrounding skin — the darkness is a scar from repeated inflammation, not dirt, and it is the reason bleaching creams fail so consistently.
