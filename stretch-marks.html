The Clinical Reality

The anatomy of dermal stretching

Stretch marks (striae) develop when rapid growth, weight fluctuation, or pregnancy stretches the dermis beyond its tensile elasticity limit. The collagen and elastin matrix tears, creating linear scars.

Early red/purple marks (striae rubrae) have active blood supply and respond rapidly. Older white/silvery marks (striae albae) are matured fibrous tissue requiring deeper thermal stimulation to awaken fibroblasts.

Because topical body creams only hydrate the superficial epidermis (top 0.1mm), they cannot repair torn collagen 1.5mm deep in the reticular dermis. Clinical energy-based devices are required.
